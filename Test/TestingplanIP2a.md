# Prototype 2A Testing Plan

## Project

**IdeaSpace XR – Prototype 2A**

## Purpose

The purpose of this evaluation is to investigate whether users can understand and successfully use the core sticky-note interaction in IdeaSpace XR.

The evaluation focuses on the interaction process:

**See → Grab → Move → Categorise → Place**

The test will investigate whether participants can organise virtual sticky notes using XR controllers without significant confusion or assistance.

---

# Research Question

**Can users successfully grab, move and categorise virtual sticky notes in an XR brainstorming environment with minimal assistance?**

---

# Participants

Target number of participants:

**5 participants**

Participants do not need to have previous experience with IdeaSpace XR.

Participants may have different levels of experience with VR/XR systems.

Previous VR experience will be recorded during the session.

---

# Prototype

Participants will use Prototype 2A of IdeaSpace XR.

The prototype contains:

* 6 unsorted sticky notes
* 3 category boards
* Problems
* Positive Findings
* Opportunities
* XR controller interaction
* Grab-and-move interaction

There are two notes associated with each category.

---

# Main Task

Participants will be given the following instruction:

> Please organise the six sticky notes into the three categories: Problems, Positive Findings and Opportunities.

Participants should complete the task using the XR controllers.

The researcher will avoid explaining exactly how to interact with the sticky notes unless the participant is unable to continue.

---

# Task Steps

Participants are expected to:

1. Look around the XR workspace.
2. Locate the six unsorted sticky notes.
3. Select or grab a sticky note.
4. Move the sticky note.
5. Decide which category it belongs to.
6. Place the sticky note onto the selected category board.
7. Repeat until all six notes have been organised.
8. Correct any note placement if necessary.

---

# Testing Procedure

## Phase 1 – Introduction

The researcher briefly explains:

* The purpose of the session.
* That the prototype is being evaluated, not the participant.
* That the participant may stop at any time.
* That interaction behaviour will be observed and recorded.

Approximate duration:

**1–2 minutes**

---

## Phase 2 – VR Familiarisation

The participant is introduced to the XR environment and controllers.

The researcher may explain basic controller safety but should avoid explaining the sticky-note interaction.

Approximate duration:

**1 minute**

---

## Phase 3 – Main Task

The participant is asked to organise all six sticky notes.

During the task, the researcher observes the participant without immediately providing help.

The researcher records:

* Whether the participant discovers how to grab a note.
* Whether the participant successfully moves notes.
* Incorrect placements.
* Hesitation.
* Repeated interaction attempts.
* Researcher prompts.
* Whether the participant successfully completes the task.

Approximate duration:

**3–5 minutes**

---

## Phase 4 – Post-task Questions

After completing the task, the participant answers a short questionnaire and several interview questions.

Approximate duration:

**2–3 minutes**

---

# Data Collection

The following data will be collected.

## Quantitative Data

* Task completed: Yes / No
* Number of notes successfully categorised
* Number of incorrect placements
* Number of researcher prompts
* Number of failed grab attempts
* Approximate task completion time
* Questionnaire ratings

## Qualitative Data

The researcher will record:

* Moments of hesitation
* Confusion
* Interaction strategies
* Unexpected behaviours
* Comments from the participant
* Suggestions for improvement

---

# Success Criteria

Prototype 2A will be considered to have demonstrated a usable core interaction if:

### Task Completion

At least:

**4 out of 5 participants**

successfully organise all six sticky notes.

### Independent Completion

At least:

**4 out of 5 participants**

complete the task without direct researcher assistance.

### Placement Errors

Participants should make:

**0–1 incorrect placements**

during the task.

An incorrect placement is recorded when a participant places a note into a category and subsequently realises that it belongs elsewhere.

### Researcher Assistance

Participants should require:

**0–1 researcher prompts**

during the entire task.

A researcher prompt includes direct guidance explaining how to perform an interaction.

### Grab Interaction

Participants should normally be able to successfully grab a sticky note within:

**1–2 attempts.**

Repeated failed attempts will indicate that the grab interaction or visual feedback needs improvement.

### Questionnaire

The majority of participants should rate the following statements at least:

**4 out of 5**

for:

* Ease of grabbing notes
* Ease of moving notes
* Understanding of categories
* Overall ease of use

---

# Researcher Intervention Rule

The researcher should avoid helping the participant unless they are unable to continue.

A prompt may be provided if:

* The participant cannot discover how to grab a note after several attempts.
* The participant is unable to continue the task.
* A technical problem prevents progress.

All researcher assistance must be recorded in the observation sheet.

---


# Observation Focus

The researcher should specifically observe:

### Discoverability

Does the participant understand that the sticky notes can be interacted with?

### Grabbing

Can the participant successfully grab a note?

### Movement

Can the participant move the note to the intended location?

### Categorisation

Does the participant understand the three category boards?

### Placement

Can the participant successfully place the note?

### Correction

Can the participant reposition a note if they change their decision?

### Feedback

Does the system provide enough visual or interaction feedback for the participant to understand what is happening?

---

# Post-task Interview Questions

1. Was it clear that the sticky notes could be grabbed and moved?

2. How easy or difficult was it to grab the sticky notes?

3. Were the three categories easy to understand?

4. Did you ever feel unsure about where a note should be placed?

5. Was there any point where you did not know what to do next?

6. What would you change to make the interaction easier?

---

# Expected Findings

The evaluation is expected to identify issues related to:

* Interaction discoverability
* Controller grabbing
* Visual feedback
* Category clarity
* Sticky-note placement
* User hesitation

These findings will guide the next prototype iteration.
