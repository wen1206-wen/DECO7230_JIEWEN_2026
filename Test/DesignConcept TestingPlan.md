# Design Evaluation 2 – Testing Plan

## 1. Evaluation Purpose

The purpose of this evaluation is to investigate the usability and clarity of the refined XR prototype on a Meta Quest 3S.

The evaluation focuses on whether participants can:

- understand what objects can be interacted with;
- use the Quest controller to interact with virtual notes;
- complete the main interaction tasks with minimal assistance;
- understand whether the system has successfully recognised their actions;
- identify remaining usability issues that should be improved.

The evaluation will use a combination of **task-based observation** and a **short post-test interview**.

---

# 2. Research Questions

The evaluation is guided by the following research questions:

**RQ1:** Can users understand which objects in the XR environment are interactive?

**RQ2:** Can users successfully use the Quest controller to select and interact with virtual notes?

**RQ3:** Can users complete the main tasks with minimal errors or researcher assistance?

**RQ4:** Is the system feedback clear enough for users to understand whether an interaction has been successful?

---

# 3. Participants

Approximately **5 participants** will be recruited.

Participants do not need previous VR experience.

Basic demographic information is not required unless relevant to the study. However, previous VR experience will be recorded because familiarity with XR systems may influence task performance.

Participants will be identified using anonymous IDs such as:

- P01
- P02
- P03
- P04
- P05

No participant names will be recorded in the observation sheet.

---

# 4. Equipment and Materials

The evaluation will use:

- Meta Quest 3S
- Meta Quest controllers
- Prototype 2 / Prototype 2A
- Laptop/computer if required for setup or monitoring
- Observation Sheet
- Interview Sheet
- Pen or digital spreadsheet for recording results

Before each session, the researcher should confirm that:

- the headset is charged;
- the prototype launches correctly;
- the controller is connected;
- all required objects are visible;
- interaction works correctly;
- the testing area is clear and safe.

---

# 5. Evaluation Method

The study will use two main methods.

## 5.1 Direct Observation

During the task session, the researcher will observe participant behaviour without interrupting unless assistance is required.

The following will be recorded:

- task completion;
- task completion without assistance;
- number of errors;
- number of attempts;
- number of researcher interventions;
- hesitation;
- incorrect interactions;
- visible confusion;
- unexpected behaviour.

Think-aloud will not be required during the second evaluation.

This decision is based on the previous evaluation, where participants were uncomfortable continuously verbalising their actions. Removing mandatory think-aloud also allows participants to interact more naturally with the XR environment.

---

## 5.2 Post-test Interview

After completing all tasks, participants will answer four short interview questions.

These questions will focus on:

- clarity of interactive objects;
- ease of controller interaction;
- clarity of system feedback;
- areas for improvement.

Participants will also provide an overall usability rating from 1 to 5.

---

# 6. Success Criteria

The following criteria will be used to evaluate the prototype:

| Metric | Success Criterion |
|---|---|
| Overall task completion | At least 80% |
| Tasks completed without assistance | At least 80% |
| Major errors | No more than 1 major error per participant |
| Researcher assistance | No more than 1 intervention per participant |
| Interaction understanding | At least 4 out of 5 participants understand the interaction |
| Overall usability score | Average score of at least 4/5 |

These criteria will be used as benchmarks rather than strict pass/fail requirements.

Unexpected difficulties will also be analysed qualitatively.

---

# 7. Complete Testing Procedure

## Stage 1 – Prepare the Environment

Before the participant enters:

1. Open the prototype.
2. Check that the Meta Quest 3S is working.
3. Test the controller interaction.
4. Confirm all notes and required objects are visible.
5. Reset the prototype to the starting state.
6. Prepare the Observation Sheet.
7. Assign the participant an anonymous ID.

Example:

**Participant ID: P01**

---

# 8. Introduction Script

When the participant arrives, say:

“Thank you for helping me test my prototype.

This is a university design project evaluating an XR interaction prototype.

Today I would like you to complete a few short tasks using a Meta Quest headset.

I am testing the design, not testing you, so there are no right or wrong answers. If something is difficult or confusing, that is useful information for my evaluation.

I will mainly observe how you interact with the prototype. You do not need to explain everything you are thinking while you use it.

After you finish the tasks, I will ask you four short questions about your experience.

The session should only take around 10 to 15 minutes.

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy, or want to stop at any point, please tell me immediately.

Is that okay?”

Wait for confirmation before continuing.

---

# 9. VR Safety and Setup

Before giving the participant the headset, say:

“Before we start, please stay within this testing area while wearing the headset.

You do not need to walk very far.

If at any point you feel uncomfortable or dizzy, please let me know and we will stop.”

Help the participant put on the headset.

Adjust the headset if necessary.

Give the participant the controllers.

Say:

“Please take a few seconds to look around and get comfortable with the headset and controllers.”

Allow approximately 20–30 seconds.

Do not explain the interaction unless necessary.

---

# 10. Task Instructions

The participant will complete four tasks.

The researcher should read each task individually.

Do not explain how to complete the task unless the participant requires help.

---

## Task 1 – Identify an Interactive Note

Say:

“First, please look around the environment and identify something that you think you can interact with.”

Observe:

- how quickly the participant identifies a note;
- whether they look at irrelevant objects;
- whether they appear confused;
- whether they ask what is interactive.

Record:

- success/failure;
- errors;
- assistance;
- observations.

Do not immediately tell them what to select.

---

## Task 2 – Select the Note

Say:

“Now, please try to select or pick up the note you identified.”

Do not initially tell them which controller button to press.

Observe:

- whether they naturally try the trigger;
- whether they press incorrect buttons;
- how many attempts are required;
- whether they understand when the note has been selected.

If they cannot proceed after several attempts, provide minimal assistance.

Example assistance:

“You can try using the trigger button.”

Record this as one researcher intervention.

---

## Task 3 – Interact With the Note

Say:

“Now please interact with or manipulate the selected note.”

Depending on the final prototype, this may include:

- moving the note;
- holding the note;
- repositioning it;
- completing the intended note interaction.

Observe:

- whether the participant understands what to do;
- accidental interactions;
- repeated attempts;
- hesitation;
- loss of selection;
- need for assistance.

---

## Task 4 – Complete the Interaction

Say:

“Now please finish the interaction and leave the note in the way you think is correct.”

Observe:

- whether the participant understands how to finish;
- whether they successfully release or place the note;
- whether feedback is clear;
- whether they ask if the task has finished.

When complete, say:

“Great, that finishes the task section.”

---

# 11. Researcher Behaviour During Testing

During testing, the researcher should avoid giving unnecessary instructions.

Do not say:

- “Press this button.”
- “You need to look over there.”
- “That is wrong.”
- “You are supposed to do this.”

Instead, if the participant is stuck, first ask:

“What would you try next?”

If they remain unable to continue, provide the smallest amount of help required.

Every time assistance is provided, record it in the Observation Sheet.

---

# 12. When to Provide Assistance

Provide assistance if:

- the participant cannot continue after approximately 20–30 seconds;
- they repeatedly perform the same unsuccessful action;
- they explicitly ask for help;
- a technical problem prevents progress;
- continuing may create a safety issue.

Assistance should be minimal.

Example:

Participant:

“I don’t know how to select it.”

Researcher:

“Try one of the controller triggers.”

Do not give the entire solution unless necessary.

---

# 13. Technical Issues

If a technical problem occurs, such as:

- tracking failure;
- controller disconnect;
- object not appearing;
- interaction not responding;
- application crash;

pause the test.

Say:

“It looks like there is a technical issue with the prototype. I am going to pause the test briefly.”

Fix the issue and resume from the same task if possible.

Technical failures should not be counted as participant errors.

Record them separately in the observation notes.

---

# 14. Post-test Interview

After the tasks are complete, help the participant remove the headset.

Say:

“Thank you. I just have four short questions about your experience.

There are no right or wrong answers. I am mainly interested in anything that felt clear, unclear, easy, or difficult.”

Ask the following questions.

---

## Interview Question 1

“Was it clear what you were supposed to interact with in the environment?”

Optional follow-up:

“Was there anything you were unsure whether you could interact with?”

---

## Interview Question 2

“How easy or difficult was it to use the Quest controller to interact with the notes?”

Optional follow-up:

“Was any part of the controller interaction confusing?”

---

## Interview Question 3

“When you interacted with a note, was it clear that the system had recognised your action?”

Optional follow-up:

“Was there enough feedback to tell you that the interaction had worked?”

---

## Interview Question 4

“What was the most confusing part of the experience, or what is the one thing you would most like to improve?”

Allow the participant to answer freely.

---

# 15. Usability Rating

Finally say:

“On a scale from 1 to 5, how easy was the prototype to use overall?

1 means very difficult and 5 means very easy.”

Record:

**Overall usability rating: ____ / 5**

Optional follow-up:

“Why did you choose that score?”

---

# 16. Closing Script

After the interview, say:

“Thank you very much for helping with the evaluation.

Your feedback will be used to identify usability problems and improve the next version of the prototype.

That is everything for the test.”

End the session.

Reset the prototype before the next participant.

---

# 17. Data to Record

For each participant, record:

## Observation Data

- Participant ID
- Previous VR experience
- Task completion
- Completion without assistance
- Number of attempts
- Number of errors
- Number of researcher interventions
- Hesitation or confusion
- Unexpected behaviour
- Technical issues

## Interview Data

- Interaction clarity
- Controller usability
- System feedback clarity
- Main usability issue
- Overall usability rating

---

# 18. Data Analysis

After all sessions are complete, calculate:

- task completion rate;
- percentage completing tasks without assistance;
- average number of errors;
- average number of researcher interventions;
- average usability score.

Example:

**Task Completion Rate**

Completed tasks ÷ Total attempted tasks × 100

Example:

If 5 participants each complete 4 tasks:

Total tasks = 20

If 18 tasks are completed successfully:

18 ÷ 20 × 100 = 90%

---

Qualitative interview and observation data will be grouped into recurring findings.

Possible categories include:

- interaction clarity;
- controller usability;
- visual hierarchy;
- system feedback;
- confusion;
- assistance required.

The findings will then be used to identify evidence-based design improvements.

---

# 19. Evidence Triangulation

Where possible, findings should be supported using more than one source of evidence.

For example:

**Observation:**  
P03 pressed the trigger several times before recognising that the note had already been selected.

**Interview:**  
P03 reported that it was unclear whether the selection had worked.

**Finding:**  
The prototype does not provide sufficiently clear interaction feedback.

**Design Response:**  
Add stronger visual feedback when a note is selected.

This combination of behavioural and interview evidence will be used to support design decisions in Design Evaluation 2.
